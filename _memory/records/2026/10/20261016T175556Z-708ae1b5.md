Pitfall: an acceptance criterion that lists a hand-picked subset of guards lets a repo-wide guard failure slip through task evaluation and surface tasks later.

What happened (agent-kit goal orchestration-evidence-collector): the task that created a new agent template listed four guards in its acceptance criteria (model-enforcement, model-currency, cursor-isolation, checks.sh). `tests/agent-kit/templates/invocation-flags.sh` check 16 bans any case-insensitive "judg" substring in live templates (agents, skills, ORCHESTRATION, models map, emitters, SKILL.md, READMEs). The template said "never judges", and an evaluator-suggested line patch added "never judge evidence". The task passed evaluation twice; the failure only appeared when the next ready group ran invocation-flags, forcing a second fix cycle, re-evaluations and a cross-task anchor rename. In the same goal, a docs task changed "six subagents" to "seven" and broke escalation-ladder check 12, which greps that literal — also outside its AC list.

How detected: implementers of a later group reported the failure as out of their write fence; a full scan of every `tests/agent-kit/templates/*.sh` then found the only other breakage.

Avoid it: for any change under `skills/agent-kit/templates/`, the kit SKILL.md or READMEs, the acceptance criteria (or the orchestrator's evidence) run every `tests/agent-kit/templates/*.sh` except emit-golden while goldens are stale, plus `checks.sh` — not a subset. Before proposing wording in a line patch, grep the guards for banned vocabulary (`rg -n 'judg' tests/agent-kit/templates/invocation-flags.sh`) and for literal phrases the edit removes.
