Decision (goal fix-cycle-provenance, user-chosen in Phase 1, refined at the final audit): when an evaluator's asks change between fix cycles, the evaluator self-labels each re-evaluation required fix with a provenance tag. The orchestrator does not diff consecutive lists.

What was decided:
- **Tags.** `[carried]` / `[regression]` / `[new]` go after the kind tag on re-evaluation verdicts only.
- **Late findings.** A late `[new]` item stays allowed. It needs a ` — missed earlier: <reason>` justification clause directly before the kind tag, is logged as a `late finding:` note, and gets no extra cycle.
- **Reversals.** A reversal (` — reverses cycle N: <verbatim earlier item>`) is a `criteria-defect` that routes to arbitration or to the user, never to a blind retry. N counts evaluations from 1, and ladder re-evaluations and audits continue the count.
- **Drift.** ≥ 2 `[new]` items across cycles 2–3 also trigger rung-5 arbitration, alongside the unanimity heuristic.
- **Scope.** This applies to Phase 4 fix cycles and Phase 5 re-audits, not Phase 3 goal re-checks.

Alternatives considered:
- **Orchestrator diffs consecutive lists mechanically.** Rejected. `[carried]` items may be legitimately reworded, and the user preferred evaluator labelling.
- **Strict mode (only `[carried]`/`[regression]` after cycle 1).** Rejected, because it suppresses real late findings.
- **Keep the justification text only in Issues found.** Rejected at the audit. Issues found has no stable per-item key, and the required-fix list is what gets recorded as the signature.

Consequences:
- The system trusts evaluator self-labelling. The orchestrator checks only that tags and clauses are present and in position, which takes one round trip that consumes no cycle.
- Re-evaluation packages must carry every earlier required-fixes list verbatim, so tagging works on stateless harnesses and fresh spawns.
- Every identity comparison strips kind tags, provenance tags, and the clause.
