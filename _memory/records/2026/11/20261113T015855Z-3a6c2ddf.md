Hidden graders written alongside a reference solution tend to encode the reference's shape, not the requested outcome. In goal agent-kit-evals, evaluators who tried two or three alternative correct solutions per task found four over-strict checks (a rename check that rejected a rename map or a deprecation helper, a test-writing check that required 3+ test methods and one import style, a refactor check keyed on .ljust calls, then a width-number heuristic that failed docstrings and constants) plus two evasions. Fix pattern: grade behaviour first; for structural checks compare normalised AST statements across functions; and make every grader evaluation include "try at least one alternative correct solution and one evasion per task". An over-strict grader biases both arms the same way and hides real differences.
