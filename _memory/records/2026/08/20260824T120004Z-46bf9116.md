## Question

After three implement→evaluate fix cycles with model rotation, can bounded autonomous continuation reduce human escalations without runaway cost?

## Load-bearing findings

- Repair-loop literature (2026 arXiv): most gains in first 1–2 rounds, exhausted by round 3–4; strategy change required, not model swap alone.
- Unanimous failure on the same required-fix item suggests criteria or evaluator defect (Power Systems Agent Benchmark heuristic).
- LLM judges are noisy; dual-judge disagreement must escalate, never auto-resolve.
- v0.20.0 shipped rungs 5→4→6: arbitration (alternate frontier family), diagnosis-first (`diagnostician`, no writes), decomposition + selective retry; bypass classes `destructive`, `security`, `infra`; max 3 extra implementation attempts after cycle 3; repeated failure signature stops ladder.
- Per-task token/cost budgets and best-of-N parallel attempts rejected or deferred (no harness token exposure; conflicts with disjoint write-set admission).

## Decisions taken

- Express ladder as markdown policy in canonical ORCHESTRATION template and feature skill; no loop driver change for Phase 4 ladder itself.
- Requirement ambiguity routes through rung 5 arbitration, not immediate human bypass.
- External dollar runaway anecdotes and Claude auto-mode stats treated as design references only.

## Open questions

- No published ladder for this exact architecture; diagnosis-first vs plain cycle-4 success rate unpublished; Cursor silent model substitution may defeat tier pins.

## Where it shipped

Kit v0.20.0: continuation ladder, `diagnostician` agent, evaluator arbitration mode, failure-class taxonomy in shipped templates.
